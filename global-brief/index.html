Nigeria · 28 November 2026

Nigeria announces 30% procurement quota for women-owned businesses

The Minister of Women Affairs and Social Development announced a 30% affirmative procurement quota at a Lagos conference on 26 November. According to the minister, women own about 41% of micro-businesses but obtain less than 1% of public contracts. Implementation details remain to be clarified.

Why it matters: Eligibility, thresholds and verification will determine how effectively the initiative expands supplier access.

United Kingdom · 28 November 2026

UK doubles minimum social value weighting for major central government contracts

PPN 026 requires covered procurements of £5 million or more to carry a minimum 20% social value weighting, focused on jobs and skills, from 1 January 2027. Covered contracts from £1 million to below £5 million retain a 10% minimum.

Why it matters: The reform connects tender evaluation to workforce development while retaining proportionality and non-discrimination obligations.

Bahamas · 28 November 2026

Parliamentary watchdog questions procurement approval route

The Public Accounts Committee reported conflicting evidence regarding whether a $413,437 direct award under a $1.564 million building-materials voucher programme received the required Procurement Board consideration. The approval route remains unresolved.

Why it matters: The case highlights approval thresholds, documentation and scrutiny of direct awards. A breach has not been conclusively established.

Trinidad and Tobago · 28 November 2026

Digital payments partnership referred to police

The finance minister reported that concerns arising from an independent legal review of the WiPay–NPICTT digital payments partnership were being referred to police for investigation.

Why it matters: The referral underscores the need for sound authorisation, procurement diligence and contractual safeguards in technology partnerships. It does not establish wrongdoing.
